01 — Web & Social Campaign 4 min read

Parks For All

A responsive nonprofit website designed to make New Orleans' park system feel welcoming, browsable, and easy to support.

Category
Web & Social Campaign
Role
UI/UX Design
Tools
Figma, Squarespace, Adobe Illustrator
Deliverables
Homepage & Key Page RedesignBefore/After AuditResponsive Desktop & Mobile LayoutSocial Campaign

01 The Problem

NOLA Parks For All works to ensure every neighborhood in New Orleans has access to a safe, well-maintained park — but the org's website and content hadn't been updated since 2018. What visitors found was a dated, text-heavy layout that made it hard to find a park near them, understand what the org actually did, or feel motivated to give.

02 The Approach

I redesigned the homepage and key navigation around large environmental photography, a simplified information architecture, and a warmer palette pulled straight from the parks themselves — greens and warm neutrals instead of institutional blue-and-white. I then built a full before/after audit, page by page, so stakeholders could see exactly what was changing and the reasoning behind each decision before it went to development.

03 The Result

A cleaner, mobile-friendly site structure that puts the mission and the parks front and center, with a clear content hierarchy for programs, park listings, and the donation flow — designed to turn a first-time visitor into a supporter in as few clicks as possible.

04 The Social Campaign

Alongside the site redesign, I designed a two-post Instagram campaign to build public pressure for the city to formally adopt the Big Green Easy master plan — pairing a data-driven hook with a direct call to action.

P4P Kicks

A bold identity system for a sneaker-focused streetwear brand, built to hold its own on a shoebox, a business card, or a hype poster.

Category
Brand Identity
Role
Logo, Brand & Illustration Design
Tools
Illustrator, Photoshop
Deliverables
Logo & WordmarkCustom IllustrationBusiness CardBrand Mockup

01 The Problem

Sneaker and streetwear branding lives or dies on shelf and social presence. P4P Kicks needed a mark that read as authentic to sneaker culture — not a generic athletic logo — while staying legible and distinctive when it's scaled down to a thumbnail on a phone screen.

02 The Approach

I developed a wordmark-led identity with a tight supporting color and type system, hand-building a custom illustrated sneaker graphic to anchor the mark and give it a signature that couldn't be mistaken for a template logo. I then applied the full system to a business card, which forced the identity to hold up at its smallest, most detail-sensitive scale.

03 The Result

A compact, recognizable identity that works equally well stitched on a tag, printed on a card, or posted to a feed — ready to extend across packaging, social, and future drops.
